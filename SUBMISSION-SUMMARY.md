# Sahaay project summary

Use this in the hackathon submission form’s “project summary” field. It does not need to appear inside the prototype UI, but keeping it in the repo makes the submitted wording reproducible.

## Submission summary (under 250 words)

Sahaay is an independent emergency-response prototype for India that improves today’s first minutes of a 108/112-style incident report. Existing call-first systems can lose time because a reporter may be injured, frightened, speaking for someone else, or unable to describe the scene precisely. They also separate the caller’s account from the information that dispatchers and field teams need. Sahaay creates a shared, human-reviewed incident picture from the first report.

In the evaluator simulation, Maya reports a van trapped beneath a fallen tree near Koramangala. One tap captures her location, tower context, battery state and optional incident media, then opens a live dispatcher bridge. Arjun confirms what is known, requests a safe camera view, gives immediate do-and-don’t guidance, records uncertain details, and decides whether Code Blue escalation is necessary. He coordinates Fire Rescue F-03 to cut the tree, Police P-12 to secure the road, Ambulance A-27 for medical readiness, and backup EMS. The occupants are evacuated safely and the case closes with no injuries.

Sahaay improves on a plain emergency call or static form by reducing repeated questioning, preserving uncertainty instead of inventing certainty, showing unit availability and distance, and making every handoff visible to the reporter, dispatcher and responders. It supports self-reporting and witness reporting, so the person in distress can still be the first reporter. Codex and Loop orchestrated CLI iteration, local validation and headless Playwright checks. All people, locations, media, units and timelines are synthetic; it does not connect to real emergency infrastructure or personal data today.
